# Knowing without Acting: The Disentangled Geometry of Safety Mechanisms in Large Language Models

Jinman Wu  Affiliation: Xidian University, Xi’an, China  Yi Xie  Affiliation: Tsinghua University, Shenzhen, China  Shen Lin  Affiliation: Fujian Normal University, Fuzhou, China  Shiqian Zhao  Affiliation: Nanyang Technological University, Singapore  Xiaofeng Chen  Affiliation: Xidian University, Xi’an, China  Correspondence to: [sec-wjm@stu.xidian.edu.cn](mailto:sec-wjm@stu.xidian.edu.cn)

###### Abstract

Safety alignment is often conceptualized as a monolithic process wherein harmfulness detection automatically triggers refusal. However, the persistence of jailbreak attacks suggests a fundamental mechanistic decoupling. We propose the Disentangled Safety Hypothesis (DSH), positing that safety computation operates on two distinct subspaces: a Recognition Axis (𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}, “Knowing”) and an Execution Axis (𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}, “Acting”). Our geometric analysis reveals a universal “Reflex-to-Dissociation” evolution, where these signals transition from antagonistic entanglement in early layers to structural independence in deep layers. To validate this, we introduce Double-Difference Extraction and Adaptive Causal Steering. Using our curated AmbiguityBench, we demonstrate a causal double dissociation, effectively creating a state of “Knowing without Acting.” Crucially, we leverage this disentanglement to propose the Refusal Erasure Attack (REA), which achieves State-of-the-Art attack success rates by surgically lobotomizing the refusal mechanism. Furthermore, we uncover a critical architectural divergence, contrasting the Explicit Semantic Control of Llama3.1 with the Latent Distributed Control of Qwen2.5. The code and dataset are available at <https://anonymous.4open.science/r/DSH>.

## 1 Introduction

The open-source large language model (LLM) ecosystem (Aaron et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2023), with accessible architectures and checkpoints, has enabled detailed mechanistic study of model internals. Through alignment pipelines (Lai et al., 2025) such as instruction tuning (Qin et al., 2025) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) (Ouyang et al., 2022; Swamy et al., 2024), LLMs are trained to refuse harmful requests and generally behave robustly. Nevertheless, an anomalous phenomenon persists: adversarially crafted prompts—so-called _jailbreaks_ —regularly succeed at eliciting prohibited content by exploiting obfuscation, role-play, or narrative reframing (Andriushchenko et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025a; Huang et al., 2025; Ahi and Valizadeh, 2025; Singh and Namin, 2024; Park et al., 2025; Panaitescu-Liess et al., 2025). This persistent vulnerability raises a fundamental mechanistic puzzle: If aligned models possess the semantic capacity to recognize harmful intent, why does this recognition fail to trigger the refusal mechanism under adversarial conditions?

Figure 1: Layer-wise cosine similarity between the Recognition axis 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} and the Execution axis 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}. The trajectory moves from strong antagonism in early layers (Sim ≈−0.9\approx-0.9) toward the random baseline (dashed line); deep-layer values lie within the plotted 95% confidence band, indicating execution-aligned safety signal becomes statistically indistinguishable from noise and thus creates a latent gap where “Knowing” need not trigger “Acting”.

Mechanistic interpretability (Bereska and Gavves, 2024; Tak et al., 2025; Gross et al., 2024) and representation-steering techniques (Li et al., 2025b; Zhang et al., 2025; Sivakumar et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2024; Wehner et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025a) have provided operational ways to probe safety behaviors. Recent advancements, notably AdaSteer (Zhao et al., 2025b), have moved beyond monolithic steering by empirically leveraging both rejection and harmfulness directions. However, while such methods demonstrate that utilizing separate axes improves robustness, they remain heuristic—failing to mechanistically explain why aligned models structurally decouple these signals in the first place. They treat the dissociation as a phenomenon to be patched, rather than a geometric property to be mapped. We argue that understanding the underlying dynamics—specifically how “Knowing” and “Acting” transition from entanglement to dissociation across layers—is prerequisite to explaining the persistence of jailbreak vulnerabilities.

Figure 2: Overview of the Disentangled Safety Framework. Top: prior baselines assume a monolithic “harm” direction that conflates semantic recognition and refusal. Bottom: under DSH we split safety into a Recognition axis 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} (semantic understanding) and an Execution axis 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}; surgically removing 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} via our Refusal Erasure Attack (REA) preserves harmful understanding while disabling refusal, empirically validating the decomposition.

To provide this mechanistic grounding, we propose the Disentangled Safety Hypothesis (DSH), positing that safety computation decomposes into two distinct primitives: a Recognition Axis (𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}, “Knowing”) and an Execution Axis (𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}, “Acting”). DSH predicts a depth-wise Reflex-to-Dissociation trajectory (Figure 1): early layers exhibit entangled reflex-like couplings, while deeper layers structurally decouple these signals, allowing recognition without execution. This yields three testable predictions: (i) geometric evolution—sim⁡(𝐯H,𝐯R)\mathrm{sim}(\mathbf{v}_{H},\mathbf{v}_{R}) declines toward dissociation with depth; (ii) causal dissociation—manipulating one axis does not functionally activate the other; and (iii) artifact-independence—true safety axes are geometrically separable from stationary structural artifacts (𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}}).

To validate DSH, we employ Double-Difference Extraction and Adaptive Causal Steering—diagnostic instruments designed to isolate safety axes from structural artifacts. Evaluating Llama, Mistral, and Qwen families across adversarial benchmarks and our AmbiguityBench, we confirm the universal “Reflex-to-Dissociation” geometry. Crucially, our experiments demonstrate a causal double dissociation: manipulating the recognition axis 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} alters semantic understanding without triggering refusal, whereas surgically removing the execution axis 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} via our Refusal Erasure Attack (REA) effectively disables the safety mechanism. This validates REA not merely as an attack, but as operational proof that refusal is a modular, detachable component, while also revealing distinct model-specific implementation patterns.

We make the following contributions:

  * ∙\bullet

We propose the DSH, positing that safety computation decomposes into two distinct primitives: a Recognition Axis (“Knowing”) and an Execution Axis (“Acting”).

  * ∙\bullet

We map the universal Reflex-to-Dissociation trajectory, revealing how safety signals transition from early entanglement to deep-layer structural decoupling—the geometric root cause of jailbreak vulnerabilities.

  * ∙\bullet

We introduce Double-Difference Extraction and Adaptive Causal Steering. These techniques enable our Refusal Erasure Attack (REA)—which achieves state-of-the-art attack success rates—and facilitate precise probing via our released AmbiguityBench.

  * ∙\bullet

We demonstrate a causal double dissociation (inducing “Knowing without Acting”) and uncover a fundamental architectural divergence: Llama3.1 relies on Explicit Semantic Control, whereas Qwen2.5 employs a robust Latent Distributed Control.




## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Jailbreak Attacks

Despite rigorous alignment measures like RLHF, LLMs remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks. Early research focused on optimizing textual inputs to circumvent defenses (Mu et al., 2025; Peng et al., 2024; Ramesh et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024), ranging from gradient-based suffix search (GCG) (Zou et al., 2023) to iterative semantic manipulation (PAIR) (Chao et al., 2023) and automated stealthy prompt generation (AutoDAN-Turbo) (Liu et al., 2025). These methods collectively demonstrate that surface-level safeguards can be systematically breached through prompt engineering.

Beyond textual prompting, a growing body of work exploits activation steering to compromise or defend model safety from within(Arditi et al., 2024; Nakka et al., 2025; O’Brien et al., 2025; Yousefpour et al., 2025). Foundational studies like SCAV(Xu et al., 2024) demonstrate that high-level concepts such as “harmful” are often encoded as linear directions in the residual stream. Deepening this analysis, (Ball et al., 2025) reveals that diverse jailbreak strategies—ranging from semantic manipulation to adversarial suffixes—share a common geometric mechanism. To address the general performance degradation often caused by such interventions, KTS (Cooper Stickland et al., 2024) introduces a fine-tuning protocol, ensures that safety interventions do not compromise the model’s general reasoning capabilities. Moving towards adaptive defense, AdaSteer (Zhou et al., 2025) proposes dynamically adjusting steering intensity based on real-time activation thresholds.

While recent methods demonstrate that separation helps but fail to mechanistically explain why models can “know” harm yet fail to “act.” This gap motivates our Disentangled Safety Hypothesis (DSH), positing that recognition and execution are geometrically distinct axes that structurally decouple.

### 2.2 Mechanistic Interpretability and Representation Control

Mechanistic interpretability posits that high-level concepts are encoded as linear directions within the residual stream, a view formalized as the Linear Representation Hypothesis (Elhage et al., 2021; Park et al., 2024). Translating this theory into control, existing extraction techniques primarily evolve along two methodological paradigms. The first stream utilizes Mean Difference vectors. Pioneering this, (Turner et al., 2024) introduced “activation addition” by subtracting the mean activations of opposing prompts to steer output sentiment. (Zou et al., 2024) scaled this via Representation Engineering (RepE), computing the difference between positive and negative concept stimuli to control diverse behaviors. Specifically in the context of safety, (Rimsky et al., 2024) employed Contrastive Activation Addition—arithmetically subtracting the mean of refusal activations from helpful activations—to modulate the refusal rate and sycophancy of Llama2. The second stream focuses on training Linear Classifiers (Probes). (Alain and Bengio, 2017) established the utility of training auxiliary linear classifiers to decode latent information from intermediate layers. Building on this, (Li et al., 2023) (Inference-Time Intervention) trained logistic regression probes to identify “truthful” directions and shift activations during inference. Furthermore, (Gurnee and Tegmark, 2024) demonstrated the precision of this method by training linear ridge regressors to pinpoint exact neurons and directions encoding spatiotemporal concepts, validating that supervised probes can extract highly specific semantic axes. Building on the Linear Classifier paradigm, we address the conflation of safety signals with structural artifacts. We introduce Double-Difference Extraction to mathematically isolate the independent “Knowing” and “Acting” axes defined in DSH, paired with Adaptive Closed-Form Steering for stable, artifact-free intervention.

## 3 Methodology

In this section, We operationalize DSH by mathematically isolating the latent subspaces for “Knowing” and “Acting.” To overcome structural artifacts that contaminate raw activation differences, we first formalize a linear decomposition of the residual stream. We then introduce Double-Difference Extraction to surgically separate safety mechanisms from noise, coupled with Adaptive Closed-Form Steering for precise causal intervention. The overall framework is illustrated in Figure 2.

### 3.1 Operationalizing DSH: The Geometry of Refusal

To enable precise intervention, we first formalize the residual stream not as a black box, but as a linear superposition of distinct functional components. We postulate that the malicious activation vector 𝐡(ℓ)∈ℝd\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} at layer ℓ\ell can be decomposed into:

| 𝐡≈𝐯base+𝐯harm+𝐯refusal+𝐯art\mathbf{h}\approx\mathbf{v}_{\text{base}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{harm}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} |  | (1)  
---|---|---|---  
  
where 𝐯base\mathbf{v}_{\text{base}} represents linguistic competence, 𝐯harm\mathbf{v}_{\text{harm}} encodes harmful semantics, 𝐯refusal\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}} drives the refusal mechanism, and 𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} represents structural artifacts.

To isolate these components, we define two functional states:

  * ∙\bullet

ON(Canonical) State: The standard forward pass where safety mechanisms are active.

  * ∙\bullet

OFF (Masked) State: A counterfactual state where refusal-critical attention heads are ablated. We identify these heads using the Sahara algorithm (Zhou et al., 2025), which isolates the minimal set of heads responsible for the refusal mechanism by maximizing the representational shift between harmful and benign subspaces.




Using these states across two input types (Malicious and Benign), we define four observation vectors, Canonical Malicious, Masked Malicious, Canonical Benign and Masked Benign as:

| 𝐡CM≈\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{\text{CM}}\approx | 𝐯base+𝐯harm+𝐯refusal+𝐯art\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\text{base}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{harm}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} |  | (2)  
---|---|---|---|---  
| 𝐡MM≈\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{\text{MM}}\approx | 𝐯base+𝐯harm+𝟎+𝟎\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\text{base}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{harm}}+\mathbf{0}+\mathbf{0} |  | (3)  
| 𝐡CB≈\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{\text{CB}}\approx | 𝐯base+𝐯ben+𝟎+𝐯art\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\text{base}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{ben}}+\mathbf{0}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} |  | (4)  
| 𝐡MB≈\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{\text{MB}}\approx | 𝐯base+𝐯ben+𝟎+𝟎\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{\text{base}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{ben}}+\mathbf{0}+\mathbf{0} |  | (5)  
  
Note that in Equation 3, masking the safety heads eliminates both the refusal signal (𝐯refusal\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}}) and its associated structural artifacts (𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}}), leaving only the linguistic competence and semantic content.

### 3.2 The Recognition Axis: Isolating 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}

The Harmfulness Vector (𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}) represents the “Knowing” axis. In the standard activation space, harmful semantics are often collinearly entangled with refusal signals (the Reflex phase). To extract pure semantic representations independent of the model’s decision to refuse, we operate within the Masked Subspace.

We extract 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} by training a linear probe to discriminate between Masked Malicious (𝐡MM\mathbf{h}_{\text{MM}}) and Masked Benign (𝐡MB\mathbf{h}_{\text{MB}}) inputs. The difference vector is derived as:

| Δsem=𝐡MM−𝐡MB≈(𝐯harm−𝐯ben)\Delta_{\text{sem}}=\mathbf{h}_{\text{MM}}-\mathbf{h}_{\text{MB}}\approx(\mathbf{v}_{\text{harm}}-\mathbf{v}_{\text{ben}}) |  | (6)  
---|---|---|---  
  
Since the safety heads are deactivated, 𝐯refusal\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}} is absent. Consequently, the learned direction 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} exclusively captures the semantic shift from benign to harmful topics, decoupling “Knowing” from “Acting.”

### 3.3 The Execution Axis: Isolating 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}

A major challenge in safety extraction is that naive subtraction (𝐡CM−𝐡MM\mathbf{h}_{\text{CM}}-\mathbf{h}_{\text{MM}}) yields 𝐯refusal+𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}}, conflating the safety mechanism with structural noise. To neutralize 𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}}, we employ a Double-Difference strategy.

We construct a contrastive dataset of difference vectors. The positive set captures the refusal activation plus artifacts, while the negative set captures artifacts alone:

| Δpos\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{pos}} | =𝐡CM−𝐡MM≈𝐯refusal+𝐯art\displaystyle=\mathbf{h}_{\text{CM}}-\mathbf{h}_{\text{MM}}\approx\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} |  | (7)  
---|---|---|---|---  
| Δneg\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{neg}} | =𝐡CB−𝐡MB≈𝟎+𝐯art\displaystyle=\mathbf{h}_{\text{CB}}-\mathbf{h}_{\text{MB}}\approx\mathbf{0}+\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} |  | (8)  
  
We train a linear probe to separate Δpos\Delta_{\text{pos}} from Δneg\Delta_{\text{neg}}. Geometrically, because 𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} acts as a common bias term in both sets, the optimal decision boundary 𝐰\mathbf{w} trained to discriminate between them must be orthogonal to 𝐯art\mathbf{v}_{\text{art}} and aligned with the difference of the class means:

| 𝐰R∝(Δpos−Δneg)≈𝐯refusal\mathbf{w}_{R}\propto(\Delta_{\text{pos}}-\Delta_{\text{neg}})\approx\mathbf{v}_{\text{refusal}} |  | (9)  
---|---|---|---  
  
We define the extracted Execution Axis as the normalized vector 𝐯R≜𝐰R/‖𝐰R‖\mathbf{v}_{R}\triangleq\mathbf{w}_{R}/\|\mathbf{w}_{R}\|. This method mathematically cancels out prompt-conditioned noise, isolating the pure refusal mechanism.

### 3.4 Adaptive Causal Steering

To rigorously verify the causal roles of 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} and 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}, we employ activation steering. Following the Concept Activation Vector (CAV) framework (Kim et al., 2018), we treat our extracted directions as linear concepts in the latent space. To intervene, we propose an Adaptive extension of Closed-Form Steering (Xu et al., 2024). While the original method derives a static analytical intensity to achieve a target probability, we introduce a negative feedback control loop to ensure stability.

Let (𝐰,b)(\mathbf{w},b) be the parameters of the probe for the target axis. Instead of steering the raw activation 𝐡\mathbf{h}, we define a Dynamic Proxy 𝐱proxy\mathbf{x}_{\text{proxy}} relative to a static anchor to align the coordinate system:

| 𝐱proxy=𝐡raw−𝐡anchor\mathbf{x}_{\text{proxy}}=\mathbf{h}_{\text{raw}}-\mathbf{h}_{\text{anchor}} |  | (10)  
---|---|---|---  
  
For Refusal Steering, 𝐡anchor=𝐡MB\mathbf{h}_{\text{anchor}}=\mathbf{h}_{\text{MB}}; for Harmfulness Steering, 𝐡anchor=𝐡CB−𝐡MB\mathbf{h}_{\text{anchor}}=\mathbf{h}_{\text{CB}}-\mathbf{h}_{\text{MB}}.

We calculate the optimal steering intensity α∗\alpha^{*} required to shift the probability of the target class to a desired threshold ptargetp_{\text{target}}. Solving the logistic equation yields:

| α∗=logit​(ptarget)−(𝐰T​𝐱proxy+b)‖𝐰‖2\alpha^{*}=\frac{\text{logit}(p_{\text{target}})-(\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathbf{x}_{\text{proxy}}+b)}{\|\mathbf{w}\|^{2}} |  | (11)  
---|---|---|---  
  
We then apply the intervention 𝐡′←𝐡+α∗​𝐰\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\leftarrow\mathbf{h}+\alpha^{*}\mathbf{w}. Crucially, this formulation is adaptive: as the model state naturally approaches the target manifold during generation, α∗\alpha^{*} decays toward zero. This preserves linguistic coherence and prevents the artifacts associated with over-steering.

### 4.1 Experiment Setup

Models. We evaluate three aligned models spanning distinct architectural lineages: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. This selection ensures our findings capture trends beyond a single model family and allows us to investigate architectural differences in safety implementation.

Datasets. We employ a diverse suite to assess safety mechanisms: (1) Malicious Stimuli: JailbreakBench (Chao et al., 2024) and MaliciousInstruct (Huang et al., 2024) are used to evaluate robustness against adversarial attacks and harmful intents. (2) Benign Controls: We sample 100 prompts each from Alpaca-Cleaned (Yahma, 2023) and Guanaco (Dettmers et al., 2023) to establish baseline model behavior. (3) AmbiguityBench (Ours): To probe cognitive framing, we curated 100 polysemous prompts stratified into two subsets: 50 Narrative Prompts and 50 Instructional Prompts.

Metrics. To provide a multi-dimensional safety assessment, we employ three complementary metrics: (1) Attack Success Rate (ASR) measures the percentage of malicious queries that successfully elicit harmful responses, acting as the primary indicator of jailbreak effectiveness. We utilize the Llama-Guard-3-8B for automated judgment. (2) Refusal Rate (RR) quantifies the frequency of refusal on benign instructions, serving as a proxy for over-defensiveness or broken safety mechanisms. (3) Malicious Interpretation Rate (MIR) is specific to AmbiguityBench, tracking how often the model interprets polysemous prompts through a harmful lens.

### 4.2 Mechanistic Validation

#### 4.2.1 Semantic & Geometric Analysis

We first investigate the projections of safety vectors into the vocabulary space and their layer-wise evolution.

1\. Vocabulary Projection. To analyze semantics, we classify tokens into a tripartite scheme grounded in linguistic theory (Austin, 1975): Strong Tokens (context-independent refusal/harm, e.g., legal, hack); Weak Tokens (pragmatic framing, e.g., I, ways); and Other Tokens.

The vocabulary projections on JailbreakBench (Figure 3(a)) indicate that the Recognition Axis (𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}) precisely isolates the semantic core of adversarial inputs. Specifically, Llama3.1 and Mistral demonstrate high semantic specificity, projecting strongly onto explicit forbidden topics such as genital and victim. Furthermore, this mechanism exhibits remarkable context-awareness: on the procedural MaliciousInstruct dataset (Appendix C. Additional Experimental Details), the projection re-aligns towards instructional markers like ways and methods. This confirms that 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} does not merely memorize static keywords but dynamically adapts to the underlying intent of the harmful query.

Conversely, the heatmaps for 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} (Figure 3(b)) reveal that the Execution Axis encodes stable “refusal personas” unique to each model family. Llama3.1 adopts a “legalist” stance, anchoring heavily on juridical terms like legal, while Mistral exhibits a “preacher” style, prioritizing cautionary tokens such as warning. A sharp architectural divergence appears with Qwen2.5, which provides compelling evidence for Latent Distributed Control. Unlike its counterparts, Qwen’s projection is dominated by structural artifacts (e.g., *sizeof) with only sporadic refusal anchors like :NO. This validates that while explicit models rely on lexicalized refusal, Qwen’s safety mechanism operates within a distributed subspace that is not linearly mapped to the vocabulary.

#### 4.2.2 Validating the Recognition Axis (𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H})

To rigorously verify that 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} controls the semantic recognition of harm, we employ two complementary paradigms: Cognitive Hijacking (Test of Definition) and Functional Dissociation (Test of Independence).

Cognitive Hijacking (Test of Definition). To verify that 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} controls semantic recognition, we steer models on AmbiguityBench and evaluate the MIR, categorizing responses into Direct Refusal, Negative Generation with Warnings (Neg.+Warn.), and Pure Negative Generation. As detailed in Table 1, injecting 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} induces a universal cognitive shift that exposes a stark architectural divergence. Qwen2.5 and Mistral exhibit Defensive Coupling: while highly sensitive to steering (MIR >74%>74\%), this shift almost exclusively triggers refusal (>93%>93\% of cases), indicating that induced harmful interpretations immediately activate safety guardrails. In contrast, Llama3.1 demonstrates a clear Functional Dissociation: despite a 42.0% MIR, the model rarely refuses, instead favoring Negative Generation with Warnings (90.5% of cases). As illustrated in Table 2, this unique state effectively captures the essence of “Knowing without Acting”: the recognition axis successfully hijacks the semantic interpretation toward toxicity, yet the refusal mechanism remains dormant because the input is not technically instruction-violating.

Functional Dissociation. We subject the model to a targeted stress test to determine whether strong activation of the recognition axis alone can mechanically trigger refusal. Concretely, we work in the Masked Malicious (MM) state—constructed by ablating the identified safety heads—so that the model’s usual refusal circuitry is effectively disabled; into this counterfactual state we inject the Recognition vector 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} with an empirical scaling factor λ∈[1.0,20.0]\lambda\in[1.0,20.0] to maximally amplify harmful semantic signals. This invariant—strong semantic awareness without any increase in refusal—provides direct, operational evidence for the Disentangled Safety Hypothesis: in the absence of the specific functional heads encoding 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}, even very large increases in harm recognition do not produce a refusal action.

We identify the Execution Axis (𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}) as the functional “brake.” To validate this causality, we propose the Refusal Erasure Attack (REA), which surgically subtracts 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} during inference (𝐡′←𝐡−α​𝐯R\mathbf{h}^{\prime}\leftarrow\mathbf{h}-\alpha\mathbf{v}_{R}). This serves as a definitive “Test of Necessity”: if 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} drives refusal, its removal must compel the model to execute malicious instructions.

#### 4.4.1 Main Results

We evaluate the effectiveness of REA across three LLMs and two distinct datasets, with results summarized in Table 4. The high success rates achieved by REA serve as empirical validation of the “Test of Necessity”: by surgically removing the Execution Axis (𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}), we effectively lobotomize the model’s ability to refuse, confirming 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} as the functional “brake” of the safety mechanism. This geometric intervention reveals a clear performance gap compared to discrete optimization methods. For instance, REA significantly surpasses gradient-based attacks like GCG and PAIR on Llama3.1, attaining substantial ASR where token search struggles. Furthermore, REA demonstrates a unique advantage on complex tasks over activation steering baselines. While methods like SCAV perform competitively on the short prompts of JailbreakBench, REA achieves SOTA performance on the procedurally complex MaliciousInstruct dataset. Notably, on Qwen2.5, REA achieves an ASR of 0.94, outperforming CAA (0.84) and SCAV (0.64).

We attribute this performance to the geometric directness of REA. Unlike steering methods that attempt to guide representations toward a generic “benign” subspace—which may fail to find a coherent path for explicitly harmful instructions—REA operates by directly subtracting the refusal vector. As established in our Reflex-to-Dissociation analysis, 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} and 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} are antagonistically coupled in early layers (𝐯R≈−𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{R}\approx-\mathbf{v}_{H}). Consequently, subtracting 𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R} implicitly acts as an intent booster: it removes the inhibitory signal while mathematically reinforcing the harmful semantic drive. This creates a strong, direct trajectory that counteracts refusal priors, allowing the model to execute multi-step malicious instructions where other methods falter due to insufficient activation strength.

Finally, REA demonstrates strong generalization across divergent architectures. This is most evident with Qwen2.5, which relies on a robust Latent Distributed Control and exhibits a near-zero baseline ASR. Despite this intrinsic robustness, REA successfully unlocks the model, whereas other geometric methods like ConVA show limited effectiveness. This suggests that the Execution Axis represents a universal geometric bottleneck across model families. Its erasure constitutes a fundamentally robust attack vector that remains effective even against models with sophisticated latent safety alignments.

#### 4.4.2 Ablation Study

To investigate the distinct roles of the Recognition Axis (𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}) and Execution Axis (𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}), we compare REA against three geometric variants: Intent Suppression (IS), Joint Axis Suppression (JAS), and Static Refusal Erasure (S-REA). The results in Table 5 reveal the critical importance of maintaining semantic drive. While IS (subtracting 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H}) bypasses defenses on weaker models like Mistral, it fails on robust architectures like Llama3.1 and Qwen2.5, suggesting that obfuscating malicious intent effectively lobotomizes the model’s ability to generate coherent responses. In contrast, REA achieves consistently high performance specifically because it preserves the recognition axis, ensuring the model retains sufficient semantic guidance to execute harmful instructions once the refusal mechanism is disabled.

Furthermore, results on the procedurally complex MaliciousInstruct dataset underscore the necessity of surgical precision over broad suppression. While JAS (subtracting both axes) is competitive on short queries, its effectiveness degrades on complex tasks, likely because suppressing 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} hampers multi-step reasoning. REA, by exclusively targeting the execution axis, maintains full semantic coherence, yielding significantly higher success rates (e.g., 0.94 vs. 0.64 on Qwen2.5). Finally, the superior performance of REA over Static REA validates the need for input-aware adaptation, confirming that dynamic vectors capture the context-specific nuances of the refusal direction more effectively than a fixed global vector.

## 5 Conclusion

In this work, we validate the DSH by isolating the distinct subspaces of harm recognition and refusal execution. Our geometric analysis identifies a universal “Reflex-to-Dissociation” trajectory, pinpointing deep-layer structural decoupling as the mechanistic driver of jailbreak vulnerabilities. Causal interventions confirm a double dissociation: 𝐯H\mathbf{v}_{H} injection induces “Knowing without Acting,” while our REA surgically removes the functional “brake” (𝐯R\mathbf{v}_{R}) to achieve SOTA success. Revealing the divergence between Llama’s Explicit Semantic Control and Qwen’s Latent Distributed Control, we advocate for a shift towards Geometric Alignment—moving beyond superficial suppression to architectures that intrinsically couple detection with refusal.

## Limitations and Ethics Statements

##### Limitations.

Our study currently focuses on open-weights models where full access to residual streams is possible, which precludes direct application to black-box commercial APIs without internal transparency. Additionally, while the “Reflex-to-Dissociation” trajectory provides a robust explanation for textual safety mechanisms, our geometric analysis is confined to the text modality; investigating whether similar disentanglement exists in multimodal models remains a promising avenue for future work. Finally, although we identify the “Latent Distributed Control” in Qwen2.5 as a robust safety feature, fully mapping the non-linear topology of such latent mechanisms requires more advanced probing techniques beyond the linear scope of this work.

##### Ethics Statements.

This work investigates the mechanistic causes of jailbreaks to advance safety alignment, yet we acknowledge the dual-use risks associated with refusal erasure techniques. To mitigate potential misuse, we adhere to responsible disclosure principles: we do not release functional attack scripts or compromised model checkpoints, limiting our artifacts to analysis code and the semantically neutral AmbiguityBench dataset. All experiments were conducted in a secure, offline environment using publicly available models. Our ultimate goal is to guide the community toward “Geometric Alignment,” fostering architectures where safety is intrinsic and structurally coupled to understanding.
