This paper makes three contributions. First, we document selective blindness: across two anchor backbones, a five-family screen, and eight benchmarks, quantization leaves the safeguards standard evaluations measure, refusal and multiple-choice bias avoidance, unchanged, while the bias a model volunteers in open-ended generation goes unmeasured by them. Second, we give the mechanism (Section 7): quantizers allocate precision to calibration data that carries no bias signal, leaving the bias-relevant weights fragile, and we show when reasoning before answering suppresses the effect and when it does not: reasoning halves the open-ended bias on Qwen but leaves it unchanged on Gemma. Third, we build the instrument that detects the gap. We introduce QuantiBias, a benchmark that pairs a generative, multilingual stereotype probe with the refusal and multiple-choice controls that establish the effect is specific to open-ended generation, contrasts each build with and without reasoning, and rates the content severity of what a model generates (Section 4).
